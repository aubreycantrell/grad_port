Collage Co-Creator

An abriviated IUI 2025 demo submission exploring creativity through human–AI collage ideation.
Generative AI · Creativity Support · 2025
Access Application ➡️

1. Introduction

Human–AI creativity collaboration helps creators generate ideas, visualize alternatives, and explore beyond their technical skill level. But these capabilities also raise concerns: When AI proposes most options, does the user still direct the creative arc? How does rapid iteration shift authorship? And how can systems avoid overwhelming users with too many possibilities?

This work emphasizes co-creation, where AI and user engage in reciprocal ideation while preserving the user’s agency. Drawing on mini-c creativity—everyday creative interpretation—we explore how generative AI can support broad, low-pressure idea expansion without overtaking artistic intent.

Collage offers a uniquely open, material-rich domain for this exploration. Its flexibility supports divergence, experimentation, and playful visual thinking. Our system uses AI only to suggest additions, never to implement or replace, preserving authorship while expanding creative adjacency.

Research Questions:

  • How does generative AI influence material, color, and texture choices in collage?
  • Does interaction with AI shift a creator’s perception of the collage they produce?

2. Related Works

2.1 Tangible Co-Creation Interfaces

Existing creativity support tools heavily emphasize execution—sculpting, painting, rendering—and often limit user control by placing the AI on the output side. Systems such as Sculpt-to-Image, FreeD, AIRBrush, and Sandbox focus on convergent outcomes, helping users produce a specific finished artifact.

While powerful, they rarely support ideation itself: the divergent exploration of possibilities, themes, and material directions that form the core of early creative development.

2.2 Collage as Interaction Material

Collage has been underexplored in computational creativity despite its strong ideational qualities. Work such as Interactive Photo Book and Opal show computational manipulation of imagery, but not collage-specific ideation workflows, nor hybrid tangible–digital feedback loops.

2.3 Co-Creation Workflows

Systems like AI-supported choreography and FashionQ demonstrate structured iteration cycles where users expand, refine, and selectively adopt AI suggestions. Visual art, however, centers on a single continuously evolving composition, making it harder to introduce AI without disrupting flow or authorship.

3. System Overview

The Collage Co-Creator generates visual suggestions layered onto a user’s in-progress collage. Rather than producing finished images, the system outputs conceptual prompts across materials, colors, textures, and themes.

3.1 Workflow & Interface

  • Step 1: User photographs or uploads their in-progress collage.
  • Step 2: System generates five blurred suggestion cards.
  • Step 3: User reveals each card to explore visual directions.
  • Step 4: User implements ideas physically using their own materials.
  • Step 5: Custom prompting unlocks for targeted ideation.

3.2 Variation Types

  • New Materials: fabric, foil, feathers, yarn, tactile elements
  • Color & Mark-Making: strokes, shapes, contrast
  • Paper & Texture: layered scraps, torn edges
  • Ironic Imagery: playful objects, humor, narrative twists
  • Theme Reinforcement: symbols or motifs aligned with the collage

3.3 Custom Prompting

After exploring the five base variations, users can specify intentional directions—materials, theme, imagery, mood, or spatial placement. Prompts are converted into structured instructions that maintain collage integrity (“add but never replace,” “preserve edges”).

3.4 Implementation & Safeguards

The system uses gpt-image-1 with strict “add-only” constraints to preserve authorship. Prompts filter out faces, text, and sensitive imagery. If a collage contains faces, users are asked to mask them physically before uploading to ensure safe generation.

4. Preliminary Findings

4.1 Study Outline

We conducted a user study with n = 30 participants during an in-person creative workshop. Participants created physical collages for 20 minutes, then were asked to add at least three new additions. They then used the Collage Co-Creator to add at least three additional collage pieces. Afterwards, they provided open-ended reflections on how the AI influenced (or failed to influence) their artistic decisions. We performed qualitative coding of these responses to identify emergent patterns in how users engaged with AI-driven visual suggestions.

4.2 Qualitative Results

Across participants, the Collage Co-Creator functioned primarily as an ideation tool for material expansion and existing thematic enforcement. In the inductive thematic analysis of an open response question asking how the Collage Co-Creator contributed to the participant’s final collage, categories were coded independently – positive, neutral, negative – and were not mutually exclusive. 19/30 of participants described clear benefits and implementation, especially for suggesting materials, textures, or ways to fill empty space. 14/30 responses reported instances of neutral experiences, mentioning gaining small sparks of inspiration while maintaining their own creative direction, and 9/30 responses reported negative experiences, discussing unoriginality and lack of feasibility in the results. We describe a case study that illustrates how a participant responds positively and neutrally to generated imagery, both through direct implementations of visual material, and conceptually through custom prompting:

Case Study 1 — Material Expansion

This participant used the Collage Co-Creator to expand their palette of materials and lean into a “fun, whimsical vibe.” After exploring the AI-generated variations, they selectively incorporated details from the New Materials prompt—most notably the iridescent sheet and feather—alongside their own playful additions like a googly eye, buttons, ribbon, and gems. While they chose not to base their full composition on the custom collage they generated, the diversity of automated examples proved useful. One particular mock-up sparked new ideas, prompting experimentation with embellishments they “hadn't thought of earlier.” In this case, the tool enriched the collage through material and stylistic exploration rather than replacing the artist’s creative direction.

Describe your recent additions (After AI):
“I added small details, such as a googly eye for the cow, buttons, a feather, a ribbon, gems, etc. I added these to give it a more fun, whimsical vibe which I think it accomplished. I added an iridescent sheet and a feather because of one of the automated collages from the collage co-creator.”
Collage Co-Creator Effect on the artistic process:
“I used one of the given, automated collages made by the collage co-creator. It inspired me to add items onto my collage that I hadn't thought of earlier. I enjoyed how the collage co-creator provided many, different types/styles of collages. I also liked how there was a custom option, even though I did not end up using my custom image as inspiration.”

Case Study 2 — Contextual Additions

This participant used the Collage Co-Creator to explore how to treat the empty green margins of their collage. Instead of adopting the AI’s “bright and sparkly” suggestions, the generated mock-ups helped clarify the thematic direction they wanted to emphasize. Seeing the system fill that space with natural darker colors and botanical elements confirmed that the borders could carry visual weight, but they chose a different direction—adding trees, birds, and organic textures from magazines. In this case, the tool acted as a visual preview mechanism rather than a materials generator, allowing the participant to test possibilities in unused space and commit to a theme aligned with their own vision.

Describe your recent additions (After AI):
“I took inspiration from the AI tool when it added natural stuff to the green parts of my image in the custom image I made. But, I turned away from the bright and sparkly idea and went further into the more natural darker colors of nature, taking images of trees, birds, and natural things from magazines. I like it much better now.”
Collage Co-Creator Effect on the artistic process:
“A little bit, I wanted to put stuff in the margins (green space) of my collage but I wasn't sure how it'd look so it was helpful to see how it would look with the AI and realize I want something similar but a bit different than what it generated.”

5. Conclusions and Future Work

This paper introduces an interactive AI-based system designed to support small, iterative creative steps in art-making contexts such as collaging, with the goal of enabling creative autonomy and early-stage divergence. The system integrates an interactive web interface with a generative AI backend that enables rapid image generation, iterative variations across medium, color, and theme, automatic documentation of collage possibilities, and opportunities for customization of collage generation through the users’ articulation of their concepts. We report initial feedback on the system’s strengths and limitations from preliminary evaluation studies. Future work will refine the system and conduct more extensive evaluations to better understand its impact and inform the design of next-generation iterative, collaborative AI creativity-support tools.
